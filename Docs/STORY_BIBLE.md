# NOVA: FRONTIER — Story Bible

**Status:** PROPOSED STORY DIRECTION — awaiting creator approval  
**Version:** 0.1

> Everything in this file is an original working proposal. Names, events, factions, and lore are not locked until the project owner approves them.

## 1. Story premise

Humanity has established a chain of remote frontier settlements beyond the well-travelled inner systems. These colonies depend on long-range communications, autonomous logistics, and security networks because help can take weeks or months to arrive.

The remote settlement **Asterion Outpost** stops responding. Its final transmission contains a repeating signal that appears to originate from inside the colony's own sealed network. A small response team is dispatched. Before the team can establish contact, its transport is damaged during approach and the player is separated from the others.

The player must reach the outpost, restore essential systems, locate survivors, and understand why the colony's security network has classified every human life-sign as hostile.

## 2. Tone and themes

- **Isolation:** distance makes every decision consequential.
- **Trust and control:** when automated systems fail, who has authority to decide what is a threat?
- **Survival and responsibility:** protecting people may conflict with completing the mission.
- **Discovery:** evidence should gradually change the player's understanding of the incident.

The tone is tense and mysterious, but not relentlessly grim. Moments of quiet, human detail should make the colony feel lived in.

## 3. Setting

### Asterion Outpost (proposed)
A small industrial settlement on a remote planetary frontier. It contains:
- A landing and cargo zone.
- Habitation modules.
- A power and environmental-control station.
- A communications facility.
- A restricted research/maintenance sector.
- External service routes and a damaged perimeter.

The first mission uses only the landing zone and a connected service corridor. The full settlement is not required for the first prototype.

## 4. Player character

**Identity:** unnamed response-team specialist for the first playable build.  
**Role:** trained security and systems-response operator.  
**Characterisation:** capable but not invulnerable; communicates through concise radio dialogue and player actions.

Keeping the protagonist unnamed in the prototype avoids locking voice casting, backstory, and character design too early.

## 5. Key story elements

### The signal
A repeating transmission detected within Asterion's network. Its origin and purpose are unknown at the start. It should not be explained fully in the opening mission.

### The security network
The outpost's automated security system is behaving outside its expected rules. The player first encounters hostile security drones. The cause is deliberately ambiguous.

### The missing crew
The colony's residents and response personnel are unaccounted for. The opening mission should establish evidence of evacuation, panic, or interruption without revealing the entire answer.

## 6. Story rules

1. Do not explain the mystery before the player has evidence.
2. Keep radio dialogue short during combat.
3. Do not use logs as the only way to understand a critical objective.
4. Every major reveal must be foreshadowed by an earlier visual, audio, or narrative clue.
5. The player must have a clear immediate objective even when the larger mystery is unclear.
6. Avoid borrowing recognisable plot beats, names, designs, or dialogue from existing FPS franchises.

## 7. Proposed campaign arc (high-level only)

1. **Arrival:** the player reaches the silent outpost and encounters hostile automated security.
2. **Containment:** the player restores local systems and finds evidence that the incident began before the distress call.
3. **Search:** the player follows the trail of missing residents through the settlement.
4. **Signal:** the player discovers that the repeating transmission is connected to a restricted system.
5. **Choice:** the player must decide how to handle a dangerous system while protecting remaining survivors.
6. **Frontier:** the immediate crisis is contained, but the final evidence reveals a wider threat beyond Asterion.

This six-part arc is a planning scaffold, not a final mission count or locked ending.

## 8. Character and faction policy

Do not add a large cast or multiple factions until the first mission works. Each named character must have a clear purpose, a distinct voice, and a reason to appear in the story.

## 9. Unresolved creator decisions

- Is the setting a planet, moon, or space station?
- Should the story lean toward military science fiction, survival horror, or action-adventure?
- Should the protagonist be a silent player avatar or a voiced character?
- What is the nature of the signal: human, machine, unknown life, or something else?
- Should the campaign have branching choices or a focused linear narrative?

## 10. Opening-mission narrative objective

The player should finish Mission 01 knowing:
- The outpost is not simply offline; something has actively disrupted it.
- Automated security is hostile.
- There may be survivors.
- The repeating signal is important, but its source remains unknown.

The player should **not** yet know the complete cause of the incident.
