# NOVA: FRONTIER — Protagonist Character Brief

**Status:** Approved format; identity details remain undecided  
**Version:** 0.1

## 1. Confirmed creative decision

The project owner has approved a **voiced protagonist** for the Balanced Sci-Fi Action + Mystery direction.

This document defines how the protagonist should function in the story. It does not establish a final name, age, nationality, appearance, voice actor, or personal history.

## 2. Dramatic role

The protagonist is a trained member of a frontier response team sent to investigate the loss of contact with Asterion Outpost. During the approach, the transport is damaged and the protagonist becomes separated from the rest of the team.

The character must be capable enough to survive combat, but not so knowledgeable that the mystery is solved through exposition.

## 3. Personality target

Proposed performance qualities:
- Calm under immediate pressure.
- Observant and practical.
- Direct rather than theatrical.
- Capable of dry humour in moments of relief.
- Emotionally affected by evidence of danger to civilians and missing personnel.
- Willing to question orders or assumptions when evidence contradicts them.

These are performance targets for later approval, not final biography.

## 4. Dialogue rules

1. Use short lines during combat.
2. Avoid repeating tutorial instructions after the player has learned them.
3. Let silence and environmental audio carry some scenes.
4. Give the protagonist a reaction to meaningful discoveries.
5. Do not narrate every object the player sees.
6. Keep mission-critical instructions visible in the objective UI and subtitles.
7. Avoid dialogue that assumes the player has found optional lore.
8. Use consistent terminology for locations, systems, and enemy types.

## 5. Voice production considerations

- Record or implement dialogue as modular lines grouped by mission beat.
- Store subtitle text separately from audio assets.
- Provide subtitles with speaker identification.
- Leave timing gaps for player control and combat audio.
- Use temporary text/audio placeholders during prototype development; do not delay gameplay implementation for final voice acting.

## 6. Mission 01 performance beats

| Beat | Performance intention |
|---|---|
| Emergency landing | Controlled breathing; confirm survival and team separation. |
| First view of outpost | Observational; note the absence of expected activity. |
| Locked relay | Analytical; realise the lock was triggered internally. |
| Drone encounter | Immediate tactical response; no long speech. |
| Signal fragment | Curiosity and concern; ask a question, do not explain the source. |

## 7. Open identity decisions

- Character name and callsign.
- Gender and voice range.
- Age and background.
- Military/security affiliation and rank.
- Personal connection, if any, to the colony.
- Accent and language localisation plan.
- Whether the protagonist's face is shown.

These decisions should be made before final casting and cinematic production, but they do not block the greybox gameplay prototype.
